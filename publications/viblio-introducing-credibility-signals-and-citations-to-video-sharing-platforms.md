# Viblio: Introducing Credibility Signals and Citations to Video-Sharing Platforms

Emelia May Hughes; Renee Wang; Prerna Juneja; Tony Li; Tanu Mitra; Amy Zhang. 2024.

Venue: ACM Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems
Publication type: conference-paper
Status: published
Preferred citation: https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642490
Website record: https://emeliahughes.com/publications/viblio-introducing-credibility-signals-and-citations-to-video-sharing-platforms.html

arXiv: 2402.17218

## Author-written abstract

As more users turn to video-sharing platforms like YouTube as an information source, they may consume misinformation despite their best efforts. In this work, we investigate ways that users can better assess the credibility of videos by first exploring how users currently determine credibility using existing signals on platforms and then by introducing and evaluating new credibility-based signals. We conducted 12 contextual inquiry interviews with YouTube users, determining that participants used a combination of existing signals, such as the channel name, the production quality, and prior knowledge, to evaluate credibility, yet sometimes stumbled in their efforts to do so. We then developed Viblio, a prototype system that enables YouTube users to view and add citations and related information while watching a video based on our participants' needs. From an evaluation with 12 people, all participants found Viblio to be intuitive and useful in the process of evaluating a video's credibility and could see themselves using Viblio in the future.

## Research summary

Editorial paraphrases with source locators; not quotations.

### Research questions

- How do viewers assess YouTube credibility, and how can citations support that assessment? ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17218v1); arXiv:2402.17218v1; author manuscript of CHI 2024 paper; Sections 1 and 3)

### Methods

- Twelve contextual-inquiry interviews informed a citation prototype, followed by a reflective evaluation with twelve participants. ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17218v1); arXiv:2402.17218v1; author manuscript of CHI 2024 paper; Sections 3.1 and 5.1)

### Findings

- Participants combined channel information, production quality, and prior knowledge, but encountered weaknesses in existing signals. ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17218v1); arXiv:2402.17218v1; author manuscript of CHI 2024 paper; Section 3.2)
- Evaluation participants found the prototype useful and intuitive for assessing credibility. ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17218v1); arXiv:2402.17218v1; author manuscript of CHI 2024 paper; Abstract and Section 5.2)

### Contributions

- A viewer-facing prototype links supporting, refuting, and contextual sources to specific moments in videos. ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17218v1); arXiv:2402.17218v1; author manuscript of CHI 2024 paper; Section 4)

### Limitations and scope

- Large-scale adoption and defenses against malicious or low-quality citations remain untested directions for future work. ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17218v1); arXiv:2402.17218v1; author manuscript of CHI 2024 paper; Sections 6.2 and 7)
