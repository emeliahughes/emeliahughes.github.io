## Author-written abstract

Each August, TikTok's For You page turns the University of Alabama's sorority recruitment into RushTok. We examine RushTok as an event-based algorithmic community: a collective assembled around a bounded offline ritual and sustained by recommendation. Using a mixed-methods survey (n=71) and a reflexive account of creator outreach, we ask who participates, how, and with what stakes. Findings show an ambiguous and entertainment based throughline; many called it a community (51/71) but few claimed membership (11/71). Affiliation centered on creators rather than shared practices, with parasocial attention clustering around a small set of potential new members (PNMs) and returning figures. Higher content exposure tracked with self-identification as a community member; those members commented, followed creators, and engaged across videos. Attempts to interview creators were met with silence or refusals, reflecting community boundary-work despite viral visibility. We outline implications for platform governance, including time-bounded context, graduated visibility, and aftercare.

## Research summary

Editorial paraphrases with source locators; not quotations.

### Research questions

- Who participates in RushTok, how do they relate to its community, and what stakes accompany visibility? ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09331); arXiv:2609.09331v1, September 8, 2026; Section 1)

### Methods

- A mixed-methods survey of 71 respondents is combined with a reflexive account of unsuccessful creator recruitment. ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09331); arXiv:2609.09331v1, September 8, 2026; Sections 3.1-3.3)

### Findings

- Many respondents recognized a community (51/71), but only 11/71 identified as members. ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09331); arXiv:2609.09331v1, September 8, 2026; Abstract and Section 4.1.3)
- Creator-centered attention and entertainment support participation without necessarily producing membership. ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09331); arXiv:2609.09331v1, September 8, 2026; Sections 4.1.2-4.1.4)

### Contributions

- RushTok illustrates a community assembled around an offline ritual and sustained through recommendation. ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09331); arXiv:2609.09331v1, September 8, 2026; Sections 1 and 5)

### Limitations and scope

- Convenience recruitment, self-report, a predominantly female audience sample, and no completed creator interviews limit generalization and triangulation. ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09331); arXiv:2609.09331v1, September 8, 2026; Sections 3.3 and 3.4)
