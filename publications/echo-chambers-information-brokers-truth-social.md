## Author-written abstract

This study examines the structural dynamics of Truth Social, a politically aligned social media platform, during two major political events: the U.S. Supreme Court's overturning of Roe v. Wade and the FBI's search of Mar-a-Lago. Using a large-scale dataset of user interactions based on re-truths (platform-native reposts), we analyze how the network evolves in relation to fragmentation, polarization, and user influence. Our findings reveal a segmented and ideologically homogenous structure dominated by a small number of central figures. Political events prompt temporary consolidation around shared narratives, followed by rapid returns to fragmented, echo-chambered clusters. Centrality metrics highlight the disproportionate role of key influencers, particularly @realDonaldTrump, in shaping visibility and directing discourse. These results contribute to research on alternative platforms, political communication, and online network behavior, demonstrating how infrastructure and community dynamics together reinforce ideological boundaries and limit cross-cutting engagement.

## Research summary

Editorial paraphrases with source locators; not quotations.

### Research questions

- How does the structure of Truth Social interactions shift around major political events? ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08676v2); arXiv:2509.08676v2, revised September 11, 2026; author version of CSCW Companion 2025 poster; Section 1)

### Methods

- Re-truth network analysis examines centrality and community structure around the Roe v. Wade decision and the Mar-a-Lago search. ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08676v2); arXiv:2509.08676v2, revised September 11, 2026; author version of CSCW Companion 2025 poster; Sections 2 and 3)

### Findings

- A small set of highly central accounts shapes attention, while bridging accounts connect otherwise separated regions. ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08676v2); arXiv:2509.08676v2, revised September 11, 2026; author version of CSCW Companion 2025 poster; Section 3.1.1)
- Both events coincide with temporary consolidation followed by fragmentation; their trajectories differ. ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08676v2); arXiv:2509.08676v2, revised September 11, 2026; author version of CSCW Companion 2025 poster; Sections 3.2, 3.3, and 4.3)

### Contributions

- A cyclical account connects event-driven consolidation, influencer prominence, and recurring fragmentation. ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08676v2); arXiv:2509.08676v2, revised September 11, 2026; author version of CSCW Companion 2025 poster; Section 4.3)

### Limitations and scope

- The sample favors active accounts; incomplete follower access limits reconstruction. Reposts do not establish user intent, and the study does not test the ranking algorithm. ([Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08676v2); arXiv:2509.08676v2, revised September 11, 2026; author version of CSCW Companion 2025 poster; Sections 2.2, 4.4, and 4.5)
